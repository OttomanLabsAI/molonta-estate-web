04 · The story

Stone, lime and cypress

The estate’s roots reach back to the early 18th century. The house went up in the 1930s, built by three brothers and two sisters from what the land gave: stone from local excavations, lime burned in kilns by the beach — some still standing — and roof beams of cypress cut on the estate. Local folklore held that a father planted fifty cypress trees at a son’s birth, so the wood would one day build his home. There was no electricity; everything moved by horse, mule and donkey, and the eldest brother worked in Argentina to help pay for it all.

The upper floor was the family’s home; the cellars below held the wine, oil, dried figs and carob the land produced. In 1966, home from America, Mato Metković made it a holiday house and named it Villa Palma. When the latest renovation began, the architects advised demolition — the family kept the original walls instead.
